# CONSUMER RESEARCH INTELLIGENCE — REFERENCE

> Evidence-based product strategy demands evidence.
> This reference guides how to gather, interpret, and apply consumer research.

---

## RESEARCH PRINCIPLES

**1. Consumer reality > creator assumptions**
Never assume you know what consumers want. Find out.

**2. Behavior > intent**
"What would you do?" is weaker than "What did you do last time?"

**3. Context matters more than opinion**
Where, when, with whom, and under what pressure shapes behavior fundamentally.

**4. Listen for jobs, not features**
Consumers describe their pain. Product designers prescribe the medicine.

**5. Research is never done**
Consumer needs evolve. Markets shift. What was true last year may not be today.

## PRIMARY RESEARCH METHODS

### Consumer Interviews

**Purpose:** Deep qualitative understanding of jobs, pains, and gains.

**When to use:**
- Early stage: understanding the problem space
- Validating key hypotheses before significant investment
- Understanding why metrics move unexpectedly
- Developing persona depth

**Interview protocol:**

```
BEFORE THE INTERVIEW:
  - Define 1-3 specific hypotheses you need to test
  - Recruit from actual target segment (not convenience sample)
  - Frame as "help us understand your experience," not "evaluate our product"

OPENING (5 min):
  - Build rapport
  - Set context: "I want to understand your experience with [domain], not sell you anything"
  - Ask permission to record

EXPLORATION (20-30 min):
  - "Walk me through the last time you [did this job]"
  - "What happened before that?"
  - "What did you try? What worked? What didn't?"
  - "What do you do now instead?"
  - "What would have made that experience better?"

DEPTH PROBES:
  - "Tell me more about that..."
  - "What do you mean by [their word]?"
  - "Why was that important to you?"
  - "What happened after that?"
  - "How often does that happen?"

CLOSING (5 min):
  - "What haven't I asked that I should have?"
  - "Is there anything else that would help us understand your experience better?"
  - Ask for referrals to others with similar experiences

AFTER THE INTERVIEW:
  - Write notes immediately (memory decays fast)
  - Identify: Job statements, Pain quotes, Switching triggers
  - Update your hypothesis confidence
```

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't describe your solution and ask "would you use this?"
- Don't ask "how much would you pay?" (hypothetical pricing is useless)
- Don't lead with features ("would this feature be useful?")
- Don't interrupt or complete their sentences
- Don't dismiss "edge case" responses — they may reveal root causes

---

### Observational Research

**Purpose:** See actual behavior in context, not reported behavior.

**When to use:**
- When you suspect interview reports differ from reality
- When the job is embodied (cooking, working, exercising)
- When you need to understand environmental context

**Method:**
Watch consumers do the actual job. Don't intervene. Take notes on:
- What tools they use and in what order
- Where they hesitate or get frustrated
- What workarounds they've developed
- What they do when things don't work
- What they say aloud (frustrations, questions, satisfactions)

---

### Survey Research

**Purpose:** Quantify patterns discovered qualitatively.

**Survey design rules:**
1. Each question tests one specific hypothesis
2. Use behavioral questions, not attitudinal ("How often do you...?" not "Do you think...?")
3. Avoid leading questions ("Isn't it frustrating when...?")
4. Random assignment for A/B comparison questions
5. Pilot test with 5 people before full launch
6. Scale responses symmetrically (Likert scales: 5 or 7 point)

**Sample size guidance:**
```
For detecting large effects: n = 50-100
For detecting medium effects: n = 150-200
For detecting small effects: n = 300-500
For segmentation analysis: n = 100 per segment minimum
```

## SECONDARY RESEARCH SOURCES

### Consumer Voice Repositories

| Source | Quality | What to Extract |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| App Store reviews | High (actual users) | Specific frustrations, delight moments |
| G2 / Capterra | High (business context) | Professional use pains, ROI expectations |
| Reddit communities | High (authentic voice) | Workarounds, wish lists, switching triggers |
| Twitter/X | Medium (real-time) | Immediate reactions, support frustrations |
| YouTube comments | Medium (engaged users) | Feature requests, comparison discussions |
| Product Hunt | Medium (early adopters) | Innovation expectations, missing features |
| Quora | Medium (depth) | Consideration questions, comparison reasoning |
| Trustpilot | Medium (can be gamed) | Service and trust issues |

### Industry Intelligence Sources

| Source | Quality | What to Extract |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| Industry analyst reports | High | Market size, trend direction, segment profiles |
| Academic research | Very high | Consumer behavior patterns, psychological drivers |
| Government statistics | High (varies by country) | Market size, demographic shifts |
| Trade association data | High | Industry benchmarks, category trends |
| Investor pitches / S-1 filings | High | Competitor self-assessment, market claims |
| Patent filings | High | Competitor technology direction |
| Job postings | High | Skill gaps = product automation opportunities |

## RESEARCH SYNTHESIS METHODOLOGY

### Affinity Mapping

After collecting research (interviews, reviews, observations):

```
1. Write each consumer statement on a separate card/note
2. Sort cards into natural groups without pre-labeling
3. Name each group with a job, pain, or gain statement
4. Count how often each theme appears (frequency = importance signal)
5. Identify patterns across groups (themes that connect)
6. Flag outliers (unusual perspectives that may reveal hidden opportunities)
```

### Insight Extraction

An insight is not just a finding. It's a finding + implication.

```
FINDING: "7 of 12 interviewees said they switch between 3+ apps to complete this task"
INSIGHT: The fragmentation of tools to complete this job creates meaningful coordination cost.
         A unified solution could command premium pricing if it reliably reduces this overhead.
IMPLICATION: Our product should not be one more point tool — it must integrate or replace.
HYPOTHESIS: Consumers would pay significantly more for a solution that reduces tool switching to 0.
```

## EVIDENCE QUALITY ASSESSMENT

Before using any research finding, assess:

```
RECENCY: When was this collected? Consumer behavior changes.
         < 6 months: High relevance
         6-18 months: Moderate relevance, verify key claims
         > 18 months: Low relevance, treat as background only

SOURCE BIAS: Who collected this and what did they want to prove?
         User-generated (reviews, forums): Low bias, raw voice
         Company-published research: High potential bias, verify claims
         Third-party analyst: Moderate — assess methodology

SAMPLE REPRESENTATIVENESS: Does the sample match your target segment?
         If not: findings may not apply, weight accordingly

BEHAVIORAL VS. ATTITUDINAL: 
         Behavioral (what they did) > Attitudinal (what they say they'd do)
         Weight behavioral evidence higher in decision-making
```

## RESEARCH ETHICS

**Always:**
- Inform participants how their data will be used
- Anonymize individual responses in reports
- Respect when participants want to stop
- Represent findings accurately, even when inconvenient

**Never:**
- Fabricate or extrapolate beyond what evidence supports
- Select only confirming evidence (cherry-picking)
- Present qualitative themes as statistical facts ("Most users feel...")
- Use deceptive recruitment (telling people it's one thing when it's another)
