**FIT EXISTS** when Pain Relievers address real Pains,
and Gain Creators create real Gains that consumers value.

## SIDE A: CUSTOMER PROFILE

### CUSTOMER JOBS

What consumers are trying to accomplish. Three types:

**Functional Jobs**
Tasks consumers want to complete, problems they want to solve.
- "I need to send money to family abroad quickly and cheaply"
- "I need to track my business expenses without hiring an accountant"
- "I need to stay fit without going to a gym"

**Social Jobs**
How consumers want to be perceived by others.
- "I want to be seen as a successful entrepreneur"
- "I want to signal environmental consciousness"
- "I want to be known as an early adopter of innovative tech"

**Emotional Jobs**
How consumers want to feel about themselves.
- "I want to feel in control of my health"
- "I want to feel proud of what I've built"
- "I want to feel secure about my family's future"

**Job Prioritization:**
Not all jobs are equal. Identify:
- **Critical Jobs:** Failure to accomplish causes significant pain
- **Important Jobs:** Strong desire but can function without solving
- **Peripheral Jobs:** Nice to have, low switching cost if not addressed

---

### CUSTOMER PAINS

Obstacles, frustrations, and risks consumers experience around their jobs.

**Pain Types:**

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| Undesired outcomes | Results that go wrong or have negative effects | "The app crashes when I need it most" |
| Obstacles | Things that prevent getting started or slow progress | "Setup takes 3 hours just to get basic features" |
| Risks | Potential negative consequences of doing the job wrong | "I might lose my data if I switch tools" |
| Annoyances | Friction that makes the experience worse than it should be | "I have to re-login every single session" |

**Pain Intensity Scale:**
```
1 (Mild annoyance) ——→ 5 (Significant friction) ——→ 10 (Unbearable, deal-breaker)
```

**Pain Diagnostic Questions:**
- What takes too much time, effort, or money?
- What makes consumers feel bad, frustrated, or annoyed?
- What do current solutions do badly?
- What are consumers afraid of losing?
- What keeps them up at night related to this job?
- What errors occur repeatedly?
- What do consumers complain about in reviews, forums, social media?

---

### CUSTOMER GAINS

Outcomes and benefits consumers want to achieve. Four types:

| Gain Type | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Required gains | The minimum expected; absence means product fails |
| Expected gains | Standard expectations; most solutions provide these |
| Desired gains | Beyond expectations; delights when present |
| Unexpected gains | Consumer didn't know they wanted it; creates advocacy |

**Gain Diagnostic Questions:**
- What savings (time, money, effort) would delight consumers?
- What quality levels do they expect? What would exceed them?
- What would make their job/life easier?
- What positive social consequences do they desire?
- What do they dream about? What would be "perfect"?
- What would be an unexpected positive surprise?

## SIDE B: VALUE MAP

### PRODUCTS & SERVICES

The specific products and services your value proposition is built around.

Document:
- Core product/service features
- Supporting features and services
- Ancillary components (community, training, support)
- Digital/physical/service mix
- Delivery mechanism

**NOT** everything you could build.
**YES** everything that directly addresses customer jobs.

---

### PAIN RELIEVERS

How your products/services alleviate specific customer pains.

**Rules for strong Pain Relievers:**
1. Must connect to a REAL pain (not assumed pain)
2. Must be specific — which pain, how much relief
3. Must be achievable — don't promise relief you can't deliver
4. Prioritize top-intensity pains first

**Pain Reliever Assessment:**
```
For each Pain Reliever, ask:
  - Which specific customer pain does this address?
  - How significant is that pain (1-10)?
  - How completely does this relieve the pain (1-10)?
  - Is this pain reliever better than alternatives?
  - What evidence supports this claim?
```

---

### GAIN CREATORS

How your products/services create outcomes and benefits consumers desire.

**Rules for strong Gain Creators:**
1. Must connect to a REAL gain (not assumed gain)
2. Required gains: table stakes — if missing, product fails
3. Expected gains: meet them, they don't differentiate
4. Desired gains: strong differentiation opportunity
5. Unexpected gains: advocacy drivers

**Gain Creator Assessment:**
```
For each Gain Creator, ask:
  - Which specific customer gain does this create?
  - How important is that gain to consumers (1-10)?
  - How significant is the gain created (1-10)?
  - Is this gain creator better than what alternatives offer?
  - What evidence supports this claim?
```

## FIT ANALYSIS

### Three Levels of Fit

**Level 1 — Problem-Solution Fit** *(earliest, hypothesis-driven)*
- Do we have evidence that customers care about these jobs, pains, gains?
- Do our pain relievers and gain creators address the right things?
- Validated through: interviews, observations, surveys

**Level 2 — Product-Market Fit** *(post-MVP, behavior-driven)*
- Do customers actually use and pay for what we built?
- Is retention strong enough to indicate real value?
- Validated through: usage data, NPS, retention, revenue

**Level 3 — Business Model Fit** *(growth-stage, economics-driven)*
- Can we deliver the value proposition profitably and at scale?
- Are unit economics sustainable?
- Validated through: CAC, LTV, churn, margins

## COMMON VPC FAILURE MODES

| Failure | Symptom | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| Assumed customer profile | Built for imaginary customer | Do actual customer research |
| Pain-reliever mismatch | Solving pains nobody has | Validate pains first |
| Gain creator irrelevance | Creating gains nobody cares about | Prioritize desired/unexpected gains |
| Required gain missing | Product fails basic expectations | Add non-negotiable features |
| Generic pain relievers | "We're faster and cheaper" (everyone says this) | Be specific, quantify, differentiate |
| Feature confusion | Pain relievers are features, not outcomes | Reframe as consumer outcomes |
